Introduction

TLDR: Pick a dataset and do a statistical analysis. That is your final project.

This project will consist of a statistical analysis presented via slides and video presentation of a health-related dataset of your own choosing or creation. The data set may already exist or you may collect your own data by scraping the web. The goal is for you to demonstrate proficiency in the techniques we have covered in this class and apply them to a dataset in a meaningful way.

You don’t have to demonstrate proficiency in every topic we’ve covered (that’d certainly take a herculean effort!). Focus on one aspect of statistical inference or modeling to answer your research question of interest, and back up your report with well-crafted, professional-quality visualizations.

Data Sources

In order for you to have the greatest chance of success with this project it is important that you choose a manageable dataset. This means that the data should be readily accessible and large enough that multiple relationships can be explored. As such, your dataset:

  • Must have at least 50 observations

  • Must have at least ten variables

  • Must have both categorical and numeric variables

  • Must be “tidy” or be able to make it tidy via data wrangling, see below.

Exceptions can be made but you must speak with me first.

All analyses must be done in Quarto/RStudio, and any results that you present must be reproducible based on your Quarto file.

If you are using a dataset that comes in a format that we haven’t encountered in class (for instance, a .DAT file), make sure that you are able to load it into RStudio as this can be tricky depending on the source. If you are having trouble, ask for help.

Choosing and Preparing a Tidy Dataset

Your project must use a tidy dataset, meaning:

  • Each variable is its own column.

  • Each observation is its own row.

  • Each value is its own cell.

For example, blood pressure measured on patients at baseline and at 12 weeks is tidy with one row per patient per visit (patient_id, visit, systolic_bp). It is not tidy with one row per patient and separate bp_baseline and bp_week12 columns, because the variable visit is hidden in the column names.

You may either:

  • Choose a dataset that is already tidy, or

  • Start with messy data and tidy it yourself using tidyverse functions such as pivot_longer(), pivot_wider(), separate(), or left_join(). If you do this, show your conversion steps in your slides and make sure they are reproducible in your .qmd.

Why does this matter?: filter(), group_by(), summarize(), and ggplot2 all work on tidy data. For example, aes(x = visit, y = systolic_bp, color = treatment) maps columns to aesthetics, so a variable spread across column names can’t be plotted until you reshape it.

Project Components

1. Project Proposal

  • The project proposal should be written in Quarto and submitted in PDF form. It should include the following components:

  1. Team Members: List the people in your group.

  2. Data: Read in a possible dataset and provide a glimpse() of the dataset.

  3. Description: Include 2-3 sentence description of the dataset.

  4. Research Questions: Write 2 research questions to explore in your dataset. Clearly state which variables, along with their units, would be involved in each question. (i.e. you should confirm that you indeed have the variables needed to answer your research question(s).)

  5. EDA: Include the following:

  • Brief table of summary statistics for all variables mentioned in your research questions (for example, mean and standard deviation for continuous variables, or a table of counts for categorical variables).

  • At least one visualization created with ggplot that explores the relationship between some of the variables in your research question. For example, a scatterplot for continuous X vs. continuous Y, a correlation plot for continous X variables and continuous Y, and/or a boxplot for categorical X and continuous Y.

  • Include good formatting: Author(s), date, and suppress excessive output/warnings/messages.

Submission:

  • Render your quarto document to html, then print to PDF

  • Submit on Gradescope. This will be a group assignment, and you’ll have the opportunity to select your group members on Gradescope. One person per group needs to turn it in. Ensure all group members have been selected when you submit.

2. Slides + Video

Your team will create presentation slides and record a video presentation that summarize and showcase your project. Introduce your research question and data set, showcase visualizations, and discuss the primary conclusions.

These slides should serve as a brief visual illustration to your analysis and will be graded for content and quality.

For submission, convert these slides to a pdf, and submit your slides on Gradescope.

Note: You will submit the video separately via Panopto, see below.

The slide deck should have no more than 7 content slides + 1 title slide + 1 team member attribution slide. Please follow the outline below:

  • Title slide

    • Project title, team member names, and course number/semester

  • Slide 1: Introduce the topic

    • Briefly introduces the topic with background context, citing at least 2 relevant peer-reviewed articles (short citations in a footer or references line are fine)

  • Slide 2: Motivation

    • States a clear, well-developed research question

    • Explains why the question matters (motivation)

    • Gives a brief narrative hypothesis: what the team expects to find and why (not a formal statistical hypothesis)

  • Slide 3: Introduce the data

    • Identifies the data source, including the website and any relevant download details
    • Summarizes when and how the data were originally collected by the data curator
    • Describes the observations (what each row represents, sample size) and the key variables measured, including the response variable

  • Slide 4: Highlights from exploratory data analysis

    • Includes at least 1 clear, well-labeled visualization exploring the relationship between variables related to the research question

    • Includes at least 1 summary statistics table (e.g., mean and SD, or counts for categorical variables) for the key variables

    • Briefly describes the distribution of the variables of interest (2-3 sentences or short bullets)

    • Mentions any major data cleaning steps (e.g., filtering, transformations, new variables) in a line or two

  • Slide 5: Statistical analysis results

    • Names and briefly justifies the statistical method(s) used to answer the research question

    • Presents the main results (e.g., test statistics, p-values, confidence intervals, effect sizes, or model output) in a clean table or figure rather than raw R output

    • Interprets the results in context and in plain language

    • Teams with more than 1 member include more than one statistical test across the analysis slides

  • Slide 6: Interesting findings from your statistical analyses

    • Presents at least one additional interesting result from the analysis, with an appropriate visual or table

    • Connects the finding back to the research question

  • Slide 7: Conclusions + future work

    • Summarizes the answer to the research question, supported by statistical evidence

    • Discusses what the results mean in the broader context of the topic

    • Identifies at least one limitation (e.g., data reliability or validity, appropriateness of methods)

    • Describes what the team would do differently or next if continuing the project

  • Slide 8: Team member attributions

    • Lists each team member and their specific contributions (not needed for individual projects)

Overall Quality (applies to all slides)

  • Slides stay within the limit of 6 content slides + 1 title slide + 1 attribution slide

  • Visuals are readable, labeled (axes, titles, units), and easy to interpret

  • Slides are clean and not text-heavy, with consistent formatting and no raw code or console output

Submission:

  • One group member should submit slides as PDF on Gradescope. Select your team members when you submit on gradescope.

  • Assign sections of the slides to “pages” on your pdf, just as you did for a lab/HW.
